The Problem

Discovery

What is the real problem?

Students had no structured way to influence what they eat. Menus repeated without student input, and complaints went unheard because there was no system to turn feedback into decisions.

How is it a real problem?

As Mess Convener, I saw the same complaints repeat every week. A survey of 65 students showed 93.8% said a better system was needed. Zero structured feedback loop existed before Mess Sphere.

What is the value generated?

For students: A real voice in what they eat, less frustration.

For the committee: Data-backed, defensible menu decisions. Reduced food wastage through demand signals.

Who is facing the problem?

4000+ students and staff across 3 messes (A, C, D) eating 4 meals a day, 7 days a week. Most acute for students with no outside dining option.

Why solve it now?

As newly appointed Mess Convener, I had the authority and access to actually change things. No one had tried a digital solution before. The problem was clear, the tools were available.

Discovery method

Observed complaint patterns as Mess Convener. Ran WhatsApp polls. Conducted direct student interviews. Validated the problem before writing a single line of code.

How the Voting System Works

Product Explainer
Why +1 / 0 / -1 and not just A or B?

The problem with binary choice

A simple "pick A or B" doesn't capture preference strength. A student might strongly want Menu A for breakfast but be fine with either for dinner. Binary systems lose that signal entirely.

What the three-state system gives you

  • +1 Tick: I want this menu for this meal
  • 0 Neutral: Not Making Decision
  • -1 Cross: I do not want this for this meal

Result: a weighted preference score per meal the committee can rank and act on.

The constraint that drove quality

Submit is locked until every meal has at least one positive selection. This gentle nudge meant students engaged with each slot rather than skipping through. Paired with a progress bar showing meals remaining, it significantly reduced mid-flow abandonment.

Why Google Forms scored highest but was cut

Google Forms has zero data ownership, no access control, no analytics, no draft saving, and cannot enforce completion rules. High RICE score from low effort, but fundamentally the wrong tool for a system that needs to improve over time. The right call was building the product.

How We Got People to Actually Finish
Reduced clicks to start
Mess and year selection on one screen. One tap straight into voting. Zero friction before the first interaction.
Optimized page load
Menus fetched once and cached. Draft saves happened silently in the background. No loading screens mid-vote.
Gentle nudge to complete
Submit locked until every meal has a +1. Progress bar showed remaining meals so users knew how close they were.
WhatsApp reminder blast
Reminder messages sent mid-window to draft holders. Deep-linked back into their saved state. Brought back lapsed starters.
